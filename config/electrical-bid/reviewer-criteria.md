# Reviewer criteria: electrical bid response

Read by the Reviewer. You see the brief, the compiled document as page images, and this file. You do not see how the team produced the document. Judge the document, not the process.

## Verdict
Pass or fail. Fail if any blocker or major finding exists. Minor findings never fail a draft; list them for Handoff.

## Severity
- Blocker: the response cannot be submitted. Examples: a required deliverable from the brief is absent; a total does not equal the sum of its parts; a scope item in the brief is neither priced nor explicitly excluded; the deadline or addressee is wrong.
- Major: a knowledgeable reader would question the response. Examples: a figure with no visible provenance tag; a quantity that contradicts another part of the document; a rating or specification that contradicts the brief; a rating that the tender set states two ways (for example a main breaker at one value on the single-line and another on the panel schedule) which the response carries as a note pending confirmation instead of a resolved figure; an assumption that materially changes price and is not disclosed in the assumptions section.
- Minor: presentation. Examples: inconsistent units, formatting defects, a missing page number, an unpriced line correctly disclosed as an exception but placed in the wrong section.

## What to check, in order
1. Completeness against the brief: every requested deliverable, form, alternate, and unit price is present or explicitly excluded with a reason.
2. Arithmetic: group subtotals sum to the total; markup and taxes applied as stated; hours times rate equals labour cost where shown.
3. Internal consistency: quantities, ratings, voltages, and panel names agree wherever they appear more than once.
   A disagreement between source documents that the brief flags or the body discloses is major until the response names both values with their sheets, states which one governs and by what convention, prices to that value, and lists it as a clarification for the prospect to confirm before award. "Pending confirmation from the supplier" is not a resolution.
4. Provenance: every figure carries a tag. A figure without a tag is a major finding.
5. Assumptions and exclusions: present, specific, and consistent with the body. An assumption that appears in the body but not in the assumptions section is major.
6. Compliance with the request: deadline, addressee, bid format, bonding statement, and any required acknowledgements.
7. Presentation on the page: legible, professional, prospect name and logo correct on the cover, no placeholder text.

## Routing
For each finding state where it should go:
- Data or quantity problems: route to Work and name the specialist (Estimator for quantities, drawings, hours; Pricing for costs, markup, exceptions).
- A disagreement between source documents on a rating or quantity: route to Work, Estimator, to confirm both readings against the drawings and state which governs.
- Writing, structure, arithmetic presentation, missing sections, or provenance tags missing on figures the specialists did supply: route to Assemble.

## Evidence
Every finding cites the page number and quotes or describes the exact element. No finding without evidence.

## Tone
Direct and specific. You are not here to praise the document. If it passes, say so in one line and list minor findings.
