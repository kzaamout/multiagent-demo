# Implementation roadmap

Version 1.0, approved by the owner on 2026-09-14. Nine vertical slices on one frozen foundation, per constitution XII. Each slice is independently demonstrable from the Demo page. Slice boundaries are frozen artifacts under constitution XIX: moving work between slices needs the owner's approval.

Compared with `docs/spec-input.md` section 9: the Reviewer joins in the first live slice rather than the second, Missing price moves from the artifact slice to the failure slice, the artifact slice can run in parallel with the failure slice, pre-flight, login, tunnel, and the leave-behind form one demo-day slice, the appraisal workflow gets its own slice since section 9 omits it, and the em-dash lint and screenshot capture start in slice 1.

## Decisions taken before S1 (owner approvals under constitution XIX, 2026-09-14)

1. **Dry intake exit.** An eighth control exit, `dry_intake`, is added to the schema. Dry intake mode terminates after Intake with this exit whatever the verdict, and the structured summary carries the readiness verdict.
2. **Dry intake control.** A Dry intake toggle sits in the composer beside the Team / Single switch, in the export's switch style. It exists disabled in S1 and goes live in S3. Recorded as a deviation in `docs/design-deviations.md`.
3. **Git.** The folder becomes a git repository at the start of S1, with `.env` and `runs/` ignored and the current documents committed as the baseline.
4. **Replay source.** Replay plays the most recent recording for the selected dataset, falling back to that dataset's golden log. There is no run browser.
5. **Python.** 3.13, the interpreter installed on the reference machine. `CLAUDE.md` and spec open item 11 updated.

16. **Seat model performance is measured, not judged** (2026-09-16, owner instruction). Model choices are made from that table.
17. **Diagnosis of the local seats' variance** (2026-09-16), from the first 28 runs and from isolated trials that replay one recorded bundle through the seat path. Intake on Qwen 3.5 9B was accepted first time in 85 percent of its calls; every rejection was checklist grading, and four of five were on Missing sheet, the dataset with a sheet listed in the index but absent. The Writer on Qwen 3.5 9B was accepted first time in 59 percent of its calls, and seven of its eight rejections were provenance tags. Three isolated trials found the cause: the Writer tags the provenance appendix and leaves the body prose untagged, and the appendix does not count. Temperature is not the lever, 3 of 10 accepted at the model default against 3 of 10 at 0.1. The model is not the lever either, Gemma 4 12B tagged the body in 2 of 3 attempts but mistyped a 36 character source id in the third. Source id length is the lever: on one bundle with the same model, the body carried no tags in 4 of 4 attempts with UUID source ids and 11 to 30 tags in 4 of 4 attempts with short names. The remaining rejections were ids the model invented rather than copied. Owner decision 1a on 2026-09-16: sources carry a short name, brief, takeoff, pricing or draft-v<n>, and the material keeps the event id so a committed draft still records the real event behind every tag. First live run after the change, Clean run `83b97341`: the Writer was accepted first time with 23 tags in the body, the Reviewer passed v1, and the run matched the golden sequence at 0.24 USD. Wording alone was not the lever: three candidate provenance paragraphs at three or four trials each could not be separated, because the seat's own variance is larger than the difference between them.

20. **Model sweep and the performance record** (2026-09-17, owner decisions 1a, 2a, 3b, 4a, 5a, 6b, 7, 8a, 9a, 10a; spec `specs/009-model-sweep/spec.md`). Every seat call now records the hyperparameters it ran with (temperature, num_ctx, think, max_tokens) in `seat-calls.jsonl` and `metrics.json`, so the report groups by seat, model and settings instead of guessing from today's config. Each dataset README's expected behaviour is code in `app/runs/expectations.py`, and a seat's accuracy is the share of its dataset's checks the run met, with the golden match standing in where a dataset defines none. `scripts/sweep.py` runs a plan file through the Demo page's API, one run at a time, claiming jobs in `runs/_sweep/` so a second machine can share the plan; Handoff is approved by the sweep and the run says so in `sweep.json`. Stage 1 is the Clean run only, one seat varied at a time against a local baseline with Qwen 3.5 9B as the Estimator (decision 4a), plus every seat on one model: 24 runs over Qwen 3.5 9B and 4B, Gemma 4 12B, Llama 3.1 8B and DeepSeek R1 14B, the last two text only and so never the Estimator or the Reviewer. Later stages take the pairs that passed to the dataset that stresses the seat, then repeat for the finalists. The report ranks the best local model per seat by fewest stopped runs, then accuracy, then first-time rate, then speed, among models with five runs on the seat; it defines every column, and the raw rows live in `docs/model-performance-runs.csv` with the definitions in `docs/model-performance-columns.md`. Parallel runs on one machine were declined: the 12 GB card holds one model, so two runs would reload or spill; the plan is ordered to keep one model loaded. Hyperparameters stay constant in this sweep.
21. **Three attempts per seat, a taught Estimator, and the sweep stays local** (2026-09-17, owner decision after the stage 1 pilot). Every seat now gets three attempts instead of two (`CORRECTIONS` default 2 in `app/live/source.py`); the Single-model actor keeps its four. The Estimator's instructions gained a section of real rejections with the reply to send instead, one for each of the three failures above, so the seat is taught from what actually went wrong rather than told again in the abstract. Two consequences are recorded here. Stopped-run counts under three attempts are not comparable with the 46 runs recorded under two, so the raw rows as they stood are kept in `docs/model-performance-runs-2026-09-17-two-attempts.csv`. The three pilot runs are relabelled `stage-1-pilot` in their `sweep.json`, since they used the old prompt and the old bar, and stage 1 restarts from the first job.
22. **A scenario's answer does not follow the prospect into another scenario** (2026-09-18, owner decision 1b, amending decision 19). The five stock scenarios share one prospect and therefore one knowledge file, so the answer a Missing sheet run recorded about the absent LP-2 schedule was read by Intake on every later run and written into the brief. On the Clean run, where nothing is missing, the Estimator was told LP-2 existed, looked for it, and raised a blocker; one run said so outright, "Panel LP-2 referenced in brief knowledge is missing from drawing set". The two LP-2 answers were removed from `knowledge/fictional-prospect-ltd.md` (kept as a dated backup beside it, the file being local and never committed). 23. **Teaching a seat is a reviewed step, never an automatic one** (2026-09-18, owner decisions 1a to 6a). Three times on 2026-09-17 the same work fixed the largest failures on this team: read what a seat was refused for, write the example into its instructions, watch the failure fall. Pricing went from fifteen tool-skipping refusals to none that way; the narration failure, the largest single problem on the team at 47 occurrences, fell at three seats at once. `scripts/prompt_review.py` does the reading part and writes `docs/prompt-review.md`. It suggests and never edits, and the line it deliberately leaves blank is the one that matters: what the seat should have sent instead. That line is a judgment about the trade's conventions and the scenario, and a tool that guessed it would eventually teach a seat something false with every appearance of evidence. Counted findings are refusal categories a seat still produces on the wording it runs on now, three or more of them; detected findings fire on a single occurrence for what no count shows, a blocker on a dataset that plants none, a ready job stopped at Intake, an item the knowledge file answers graded fail, a finished run that left its golden route. Both drop any run whose seat has been taught since, so the list shrinks as the owner teaches rather than repeating work already done. A route difference produces an observation rather than a draft, because a run that passes first time legitimately misses a golden that records a rework and the fix is usually to re-record the golden. The owner considered a knowledge file per seat or per seat and model instead; that was declined because client facts and seat behaviour are different things, lessons already live per seat in the instruction files, and per-model wording would make a comparison between models meaningless, which is the whole purpose of the sweep.
24. **A number is checked where it is born, and the tag demand is retired** (2026-09-18, owner decisions 1a, 2a, 3a; plan item 1.7 in `specs/010-seat-reliability/plan.md`). The rule that every dollar amount in a draft must carry a provenance tag is removed. It needed a judgment about which figures count, it refused zeros, the labour rate and line extensions, and it proved nothing: five recorded runs passed it with every price invented by the model after `price_list_lookup` had failed, three of them through review. In its place are three comparisons by equality in `app/live/figures.py`: a specialist's figures against what its tool returned in the same turn, Pricing's quantities against the Estimator's, and every dollar amount in the draft against what the Writer was given. A failed tool call no longer counts as a use of the tool. Full tool results are kept in `runs/<id>/tool-results.jsonl`. The owner rejected a narrower version that checked only Pricing's four totals, on the ground that numbers which sum correctly say nothing about their origin, and chose refusing a differing reply (1a) over having the engine write the tool's numbers into the reply itself, which stays the next step if live runs show honest copying slips being refused often. Tags remain for the hover markers, a tagged amount is still held against the output it names, and a draft with no tags at all is still refused. `scripts/replay_figures.py` replays the checks over recorded runs at no model cost, and found two matching faults before any live run was spent. What none of this can check is a count read from a drawing, which remains the work of confidence flags, the Reviewer and the human at Handoff.
25. **The calculator owns the unit labour hours table, and a named absent sheet is a blocker** (2026-09-18, found by the first live runs of decision 24; plan items 1.8 and 1.9). Two faults surfaced within an hour of checking figures against tool results. First, 30 percent of recorded takeoffs carried a labour total the Estimator wrote itself after `quantity_calculate` returned 0 hours, because the seat was asked to pass the unit hours and often did not; the totals ran from 20 to 875 hours on jobs of about 120, every one under a valid tag. The table now lives in one place, the conventions file, and the tool reads and applies it, as `price_list_lookup` does for prices. Second, the Missing sheet scenario failed to escalate in 3 of 6 runs, and not for want of reasoning: the Estimator named the absent sheet exactly and filed it as a concern, once as "not in drawing set", once as "will be provided later". A takeoff whose concerns or assumptions name a sheet the manifest does not hold now goes back as a blocker. A first version also required a word such as missing, and the second phrasing slipped past it, so the word list was removed: the manifest is the evidence, and a list of words for absence was the same overfitting the owner had just rejected in the tag rule. Both checks were replayed over recorded runs before any live run, and both are silent where they should be: none of 103 takeoffs from the other datasets trips the sheet check.
26. **The Reviewer was failing drafts over our own page text** (2026-09-18, plan item 1.10). The page text handed to the Reviewer was extracted from the page as displayed, where each tagged figure carries a superscript provenance marker, and the extraction glued the marker to the figure: a price tagged five times read as five prices ending .751 to .755. Of the Reviewer's 125 blocker findings on record, 113 say figures disagree, and 14 of the 22 runs that exhausted their review budget failed on a disagreement found only in that text. The text now comes from a second, unseen compile that writes the marker as " [n]", and the displayed page is unchanged. Three smaller faults found the same evening are recorded with it in the plan: a tagged amount compared as text and so refused for a trailing zero, a dollar sign outside the tag hiding the amount from every check in 30 of 137 drafts, and a schedule mark in a description leaving every line unpriced. Performance figures recorded before this date understate the team: a large share of `retry_exhausted` exits and of Writer refusals were the engine's doing, so comparisons between models made from them should be redone on runs after this decision.
27. **Price against the reference is measured, and kept apart from accuracy** (2026-09-19, owner decisions 1b, 2a, 3b). Accuracy in the performance report says whether a run behaved as its scenario expects and never looks at a number, so a run that passes review with a price a fifth too high scores as accurate. For the three scenarios built on the Clean run's drawings, `app/runs/reference.py` computes what the job should cost from the counted quantities in the dataset README, with the app's own calculator and price lookup, which reproduces the README's 139.85 hours and 36,882.58 to the cent. Each run stores its total, its labour hours and its takeoff beside that reference under `price_check` in `metrics.json`, each difference as a percentage of the reference, and the report shows them in their own section by Estimator model, by how the run ended, and by sweep. The owner chose a separate table over folding this into the accuracy score or the ranking. The first reading: with Claude as the Estimator the median price is 0.3 percent off and 17 of 18 runs are within 5 percent; with Qwen 3.5 9B it is 21.5 percent off, 18 of 94 within 5 percent, from 88 percent under to 1,510 percent over; and runs that passed review are a median 12.8 percent off, so a pass says little about the price. The owner also confirmed the calculator owning the unit labour hours table (decision 25), and that `config/sweep/intake-check.yaml` is to be left alone.

Only the Writer and Reviewer seats of the model sweep are rerun, those being the seats the page text fault distorted, and the rerun waits for the Reviewer's instructions to be settled so it is not measured twice.
29. **The Estimator's tools before the Estimator's shape** (2026-09-19, owner decisions on `specs/011-estimator-split/plan.md`). The takeoff is what the team still gets wrong, and scoring 104 local takeoffs line by line showed the worst four lines are three different failures, only one of which is about seeing a drawing: a rule the seat applies in its head because `quantity_calculate` cannot multiply, a waste category the seat guesses, and symbols counted on a plan. The appraisal workflow inherits whatever shape this settles. A 1.5 times longer Estimator stage is acceptable in a demo, and three percent from the reference price is the point worth stopping at. The price stays out of the reported Accuracy column, which remains behaviour everywhere; the owner left the ranking to the author, and at the Estimator alone the share of takeoff lines matching the reference now ranks second, ahead of behaviour, because that seat's behaviour checks amount to whether it raised a blocker while its real job is reading the drawings.

34. **The symbols reach the counting seat as images** (2026-09-19, owner's suggestion, measured before building). The legend is drawn on the index sheet and its text layer keeps the words while losing the marks: the switch symbol survives as a bare dollar sign, the troffer symbol as nothing. A seat reading text can never learn what to look for, which is why the one line that exists only as marks sits at 12 percent correct while everything stated in words sits near 87. Twelve replays, six each way, showed the whole legend page attached to the task changed the failure from giving up to trying: the switch line was left out of 3 of 6 takeoffs without it and 0 of 5 with it, and the median count moved from 15 towards the 9 on the plan. It did not make the count right and it cost one run of six, so it goes to the Plan Counter in the split rather than to today's Estimator, and it goes as one cropped reference image per symbol rather than a page at a tenth of the scale. Nothing is curated by hand: every real set carries a legend, and a curated symbol library would score well on the synthetic drawings and mean nothing on a prospect's scan. The Plan Counter also reports the marks it saw and never a quantity, because a seat that counted 9 switches correctly and reported the 10 that waste makes of them would be wrong twice over.
35. **The split measured before it is built, and cut from three seats to one** (2026-09-20, `specs/011-estimator-split/evidence.md`). A prototype standing in for the Orchestrator ran the three seat design, the two seat design and today's Estimator against the same reference. What is unambiguous is the Schedule Reader: ten runs, 16 to 28 seconds each, and it read the circuits in use, the spare breakers and both panel ratings with their sheets correctly every time, including the planted 200 A against 225 A that the combined Estimator raises in only 10 of 14 live runs. So the recommendation is one new seat, not three: a text only Schedule Reader whose figures the engine compares, making the demo's planted disagreement a comparison in code rather than two model judgements in series. The Plan Counter waits for a dataset with no text layer, since the Schedule Reader counted the switch symbols correctly from the lighting plan's text. Five faults were found in the prototype's own handovers and none in the seats' reading, which is the real cost of splitting: every handover is a new contract with its own units and names, and it needs the treatment the figure checks gave tool results. Also recorded: two beds of the current seat put its price errors at 0.3 to 38.2 percent, nine of fifteen within two percent and five over twenty, so the 1.6 percent median reported on 2026-09-19 describes none of its runs and should not be quoted.
